# Kestrel Vale Health Partners — Patient Records and Clinical Safety Standard

Standard KV-STD-3 · Revision: v2 · Effective: 2026-08-01 · Owner: Caldicott Guardian, with the Clinical Safety Officer

## Purpose and scope

Kestrel Vale Health Partners runs eleven community clinics, a minor injuries unit and a
home-visiting nursing service. This standard applies to everyone who works for the network,
including locum and agency staff, and to every system that holds or shows patient
information, on paper or on screen.

Each obligation below is binding. Its reference is the text in bold at the start of the
paragraph; quote that reference when you raise a question about it.

## Definitions

- *Chart*: everything the network holds about one patient, in any system.
- *Care relationship*: a clinician is in one while they are treating the patient, are booked
  to see them, or are acting on a referral, result or message about them.
- *Privacy officer*: the person the Caldicott Guardian names to oversee access to charts.
- *Clinical safety officer*: the clinician accountable for the safety of clinical systems.

## Part A — Knowing who the patient is

**ID-01** Before a chart is opened for a patient who is present, staff confirm two identifiers with the patient: full name and date of birth.

**ID-02** Two charts are combined into one only after two members of staff have each confirmed that they belong to the same person, and a combination can be undone for ninety days afterwards.

**ID-03** At the start of every video or telephone consultation, the clinician confirms who they are speaking to with a one-time code sent to the mobile number held on the chart.

**ID-04** A patient may be known by a preferred name, but their legal name remains on the chart and is the name printed on prescriptions and referral letters.

**ID-05** A chart opened for a newborn carries a temporary identifier, which is replaced by the permanent national health number within fourteen days of birth.

## Part B — Who may read a chart

**RA-01** A clinician opens a patient's chart only while they are taking part in that patient's care, apart from the recorded emergency access that RA-02 allows.

**RA-02** Opening the chart of a patient with whom the user has no current care relationship requires a reason typed before the chart opens, and every such opening is reviewed by the privacy officer within five working days.

**RA-03** Reception and billing roles see contact, appointment and payment details only; they never see clinical notes, results or diagnoses.

**RA-04** The charts of employees who are also patients, and of anyone the privacy officer marks as a public figure, are restricted, and each opening of a restricted chart is reported to the privacy officer on the same day.

**RA-05** Computers in consulting rooms lock their screen after five minutes without keyboard or mouse input.

## Part C — Consent and sharing

**CS-01** Information about a patient leaves the network only with the patient's recorded consent, unless a law compels the disclosure.

**CS-02** A patient may withdraw consent at any time, and the withdrawal applies to every disclosure made from one working day after it is received.

**CS-03** Agreement to be contacted by text message or email is recorded on its own, apart from agreement to treatment.

**CS-04** Nothing about a patient aged sixteen or seventeen is disclosed to a parent or guardian without the young person's agreement, unless the treating clinician records a safeguarding reason.

**CS-05** Chart data used for research needs the approval of the ethics committee, and names, addresses and health numbers are removed before the data leaves the clinical system.

## Part D — Medicines

**MS-01** Every prescription is checked against the allergies recorded on the chart before it is issued, and overriding an allergy warning needs a reason entered by the prescriber.

**MS-02** Controlled drugs are prescribed only by a clinician who holds the matching prescribing authority, and every controlled drug issued is entered in the controlled drugs register.

**MS-03** A repeat prescription runs for at most six months before a clinician must review it.

**MS-04** Doses for children are worked out from a weight measured within the previous thirty days.

**MS-05** A medicine warning shown to a prescriber can be acknowledged for the prescription in hand only; it can never be switched off for later prescriptions.

## Part E — Talking to patients

**CM-01** A test result is released to the patient portal only once the clinician who ordered it has reviewed it, except results the laboratory marks as routine and normal.

**CM-02** A text message or email to a patient says nothing clinical beyond the time and place of an appointment, unless the patient has chosen to receive detailed messages.

**CM-03** Appointment reminders go out at least seventy-two hours before the appointment.

**CM-04** A copy of every letter and message sent to a patient is kept on their chart.

**CM-05** Any conversation that seeks consent from a patient who prefers a language other than English uses a qualified interpreter, never a relative or friend.

## Part F — Keeping records

**RK-01** An adult's chart is kept for eight years after the last contact with the network.

**RK-02** A child's chart is kept until the patient's twenty-fifth birthday, or for eight years after their death if that comes first.

**RK-03** A clinical entry is never deleted or overwritten; a correction is added as a new entry showing the original text, who corrected it and why.

**RK-04** A patient's request for a copy of their own chart is answered within twenty-one calendar days.

**RK-05** Paper documents scanned onto a chart are checked for legibility, and the paper is kept for three months before it is shredded.

## Part G — When something goes wrong

**IR-01** Any event that harmed a patient, or could have, is reported in the incident system within twenty-four hours of someone noticing it.

**IR-02** A patient harmed by their care is told what happened by a senior clinician within ten working days.

**IR-03** A fault in clinical software that affected care is reported to the clinical safety officer as an incident.

**IR-04** Any change to a clinical system that alters what clinicians see on screen, or the order of steps in a clinical task, is assessed by the clinical safety officer before release, and the assessment is filed in the hazard log.

**IR-05** A suspected loss or disclosure of patient information is reported to the privacy officer within two hours.

## Part H — Suppliers and devices

**TP-01** A company that will handle patient information for the network signs a data processing agreement before it receives any.

**TP-02** Patient information is stored only in data centres located inside the country.

**TP-03** A medical device is entered in the device inventory, with its owner and software version, before it is connected to the network.

**TP-04** Laptops, tablets and phones used for clinical work encrypt their storage and can be wiped remotely.

**TP-05** Patient information is not carried on USB drives or other removable media, except encrypted drives issued by the IT service desk.

## Questions and exceptions

An exception to any obligation is granted only in writing by the Caldicott Guardian, for a
named system and a stated period. Raise questions with the privacy officer.
