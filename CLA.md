# METIS Individual Contributor Licence Agreement (v1.0)

**Before you read the terms, read why they exist.** The rationale is in
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#why-there-is-a-cla) and it is short: this agreement
exists so that Zylos Labs LLC can change METIS's licence in the future without having
to find and ask every person who ever contributed to it. That is the whole purpose. It
is not to support a paid edition — there is no paid edition, and no plan for one.

Some people decline to sign a CLA on principle, and that is a reasonable position. It
is better that you decide that now than discover the reason afterwards.

pairs correctly with a permissive outbound licence. It does not pair with a copyleft
one: a direct, condition-free grant from You to every recipient would let a recipient
take Your Contribution on those terms instead of on METIS's, and the copyleft would not
reach it. Removing the downstream grant is what keeps AGPL-3.0 meaning what it says.

## What this means in practice

- **You keep your copyright.** You are granting a licence, not assigning ownership.
  Your Contribution remains yours to use, sell or relicense anywhere else.
- **Zylos Labs LLC may relicense METIS**, including your Contribution, on terms of its
  choosing. That is clause 2, stated plainly, and it is the reason this Agreement
  exists.
- **What is already published stays published on the terms it was published under.**
  That is not a promise added here; it follows from AGPL-3.0 §2, under which the
  rights granted to anyone who has received a release "are irrevocable provided the
  stated conditions are met". A future relicence changes the terms of future
  distribution. It cannot reach back and withdraw a licence someone already has.
- **It does not give recipients anything directly from you.** Clause 4.

## How to sign

Open a pull request. A bot will comment on it with a link to this document and ask you
to reply, on that pull request, with:

> I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA

Your signature — your GitHub username, user id, pull-request number and timestamp — is
recorded in `signatures/version1/cla.json` on the `cla-signatures` branch of this
repository. You sign once; the bot recognises you on every pull request after that.

If you are contributing as part of your employment, your employer probably also needs
to sign the [Corporate CLA](CLA-CORPORATE.md). Clause 5 is the test.
